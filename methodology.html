The SOLVE Methodology

How the framework works, how conversations are scored, and why an 85 means something.

Most sales training gives you techniques. SOLVE gives you a sequence, a standard, and a way to measure whether you actually did it. This page explains all three.

The Five Steps

SOLVE is a five-step consultative sequence

The order matters. Each step earns the right to the next one, and skipping one is what creates every stall and objection later.

S

Situation

Understand the situation before you say a word about your product. Who is this person, what is actually going on in their life or their business, and why are they having this conversation today? Most professionals skip straight to what they sell. The situation is the ground everything else stands on.

O

Open with Questions

Lead with curiosity, not a pitch. Your first move is a question, not a presentation. Open questions surface information that closed questions never reach, and they signal to the other person that you are trying to understand them rather than process them.

L

Listen for the Outcome

Hear the future they are actually trying to reach. The product is never the point. Nobody wants a drill; they want the hole. Nobody wants a loan; they want the house, the security, the payment they can live with. Listen past the request to the outcome behind it.

V

Visualize Success

Help them picture what "solved" looks like before you propose anything. When someone can see the outcome clearly, the decision stops feeling like a risk and starts feeling obvious. Skip this step and you get "let me think about it."

E

Engineer the Solution

Build the answer around what they told you, even when the honest answer is not you. Engineering means constructing a real solution to a real problem, with honest expectations, not steering them toward whatever you happened to have available.

The Scoring Rubric

Every conversation is scored against the same five categories

This rubric is published on purpose. Your reps should know exactly what they are being measured on.

Needs discovery

The drill vs. the hole. Did the consultant uncover what the customer is actually trying to accomplish, not just what they asked for? Surface-level order-taking scores low here. Genuine curiosity about the underlying situation scores high.

Objection prevention via early discovery

Objections are diagnostics, not obstacles. This category measures whether the consultant surfaced and resolved concerns early through good questions, rather than waiting to fight them at the end. "Too expensive" late in a conversation usually means Visualize Success never happened.

Trust building

Did the consultant earn the right to ask harder questions? Did the customer open up, or stay guarded? Trust is not politeness. It is whether the other person felt understood enough to tell you the truth.

Natural, decision-focused close

Did the conversation move toward a real decision without pressure? A good close is not a technique. It is the natural next step when someone genuinely understands their options and feels good about one of them.

Relationship continuity / follow-up

Did the conversation end with a concrete, mutual next step, or did it end as a handoff? This is where most conversations quietly leak value.

Where It Stalled

Every scored conversation names the earliest SOLVE step that broke down

This matters more than the number.

A score tells you how it went. "Where it stalled" tells you what to fix. If a conversation stalls at Listen for the Outcome, no amount of closing practice will help, the problem happened three steps earlier. Coaching starts with a diagnosis instead of a debate, and reps stop guessing at what went wrong.

The Standard

To advance a level, a consultant must score 85 or better

That threshold is deliberate, and it is not easy.

85%
Minimum score to advance a level

An 85 means the consultant genuinely uncovered the customer's real situation and needs, not that they were pleasant or smooth. A friendly, well-mannered conversation that never digs in will not reach 85. A rep who never uncovers the real motivation cannot score above 85 no matter how good they sounded, because the rubric weights discovery heavily on purpose.

Difficulty also rises with each level. Beginner conversations build fundamentals. Intermediate adds real objections and harder personalities. Advanced runs full discovery-to-decision conversations under pressure. The same 85 threshold applies at each level, but what it takes to earn one keeps getting harder.

There are no easy 85s. That is the point.

Why the Score Is Consistent

This is where SOLVE differs from asking a general-purpose AI to grade a call

Ask ChatGPT or any generic chatbot to score the same conversation twice and you will get two different answers, because there is no fixed standard behind it. It is grading against a general impression of what a good conversation sounds like, and it is agreeable by design. It will tell you the call was great if you seem to want to hear that.

SOLVE Coach™ measures every conversation against one fixed, published rubric, the same five steps and five categories on this page. The same conversation returns the same score. An 85 on a Tuesday phone call means the same thing as an 85 on a certification exam. That consistency is what makes the score usable for certification, for coaching, and for tying advancement to demonstrated skill.

A grade you cannot reproduce is an opinion. A score you can reproduce is a measurement.

Conversations That Never Repeat

Engineered around an outcome. Never the same conversation twice.

Every practice Conversation is engineered around a defined ideal outcome, but the customer's personality, motivations, and objections vary within it. The same scenario never plays the same way twice.

This is deliberate. If reps could memorize a path to an 85, the score would measure recall instead of skill. Instead they have to think their way there every time, reading a new person, asking real questions, and adapting. That is what makes the practice transfer to real customers.
